Step 1. Build the individual lactate profile

iTRIMP weights every heart rate sample by the player's own blood lactate response. Enter the HR and lactate at the end of each stage of an incremental test. The calculator converts HR to a fraction of heart rate reserve (ΔHR ratio), fits the exponential curve lactate = A · eB · ΔHR and gives you the two constants A and B.

Step 4. Build a training week

Add every session file from one week, set the player and session type for each, then calculate them all. The weekly summary compares each player with the population guidelines of about 450 to 500 AU iTRIMP and about 30 (± 20) minutes above 90% HRmax per week (Buchheit et al., 2025). Match and training minutes are shown separately because match HR can overstate the aerobic demand.

The ΔHR ratio

Every TRIMP based on heart rate reserve starts by expressing HR as a fraction of the range between rest and maximum.

ΔHR ratio = (HR − HRrest) ÷ (HRmax − HRrest)

The TRIMP methods

MethodHow it is calculated hereIndividualised
iTRIMP (Manzi et al., 2009; Akubat et al., 2012)Sum over every sample of time (min) × ΔHR × A · eB · ΔHR, where A and B come from the player's own lactate profileYes
bTRIMP (Banister, 1991)Duration (min) × mean ΔHR × 0.64 · e1.92 · mean ΔHR for males, or 0.86 · e1.67 · mean ΔHR for femalesNo
eTRIMP (Edwards, 1993)Minutes in 50–60, 60–70, 70–80, 80–90 and 90–100% HRmax × 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5No
luTRIMP (Lucía et al., 2003)Minutes below LT × 1, between LT and OBLA × 2, above OBLA × 3Partly
Stagno TRIMP (Stagno, Thatcher and van Someren, 2007)Minutes in 65–71, 72–78, 79–85, 86–92 and 93–100% HRmax × 1.25, 1.71, 2.54, 3.61 and 5.16No (team curve)
Time in HR zoneMinutes at or above 80, 85 and 90% HRmaxDepends on HRmax

Why the weekly picture matters

iTRIMP works best as a macro-level measure of cumulative cardiovascular load across a week or block, while time above 90% HRmax works as a micro-level target you can prescribe within a session. Two sessions can produce the same TRIMP from very different intensity distributions, so look at both numbers together (Buchheit et al., 2025).

File formats

  • One column of HR values. The sampling rate you choose sets the time between samples.
  • Time and HR columns, with or without a header. Time can be seconds, mm:ss or h:mm:ss.ms. The real gap between samples is used, so breath-by-breath CPET data and 1 Hz chest strap data are both handled correctly.
  • Exports with information rows at the top (Polar, CPET software). The calculator searches for a header such as HR, HR (bpm) or Heart rate and reads the data below it.
  • Values below 30 or above 240 bpm are treated as dropouts and skipped. Gaps longer than five times the usual sampling interval are not counted as exercise time.
How A and B are fitted

The calculator takes the natural log of each lactate value and fits a straight line to ln(lactate) against ΔHR ratio. The slope is B and A = eintercept. This is the same method Excel uses for an exponential trendline, so your values should match a spreadsheet fit. HR at 2 and 4 mmol·L⁻¹ are found by straight-line interpolation between the two stages either side of each value.

Note that some TRIMP spreadsheets use 2.712 in place of e (2.71828). That small difference changes iTRIMP slightly, so values here can sit a little above a spreadsheet that uses 2.712.

Privacy

Files are read by your browser and never uploaded anywhere. Players are stored in this browser's local storage. Clearing browser data erases them, so export a copy if you need to keep it.
