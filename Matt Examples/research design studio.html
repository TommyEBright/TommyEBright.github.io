Step 1 · Approach

What kind of question are you asking?

Every research design starts from the question. Choose the approach that matches what you want to find out. The question builder, feedback and method advice all adapt to it, and you can switch at any time.

Quantitative

Measure, compare and test.

Asks
How much? Is there a difference? Are two things related? Does something have an effect?
Questions often start
“Is there a difference in…”, “What is the relationship between…”, “What effect does…”
Data
Numbers: scores, times, counts, ratings from validated scales.
Sample
Larger groups, with the same measures for everyone.
You can claim
Patterns, differences, relationships and effects, with a stated level of confidence.

Qualitative

Explore, understand and interpret.

Asks
What is it like? How do people make sense of it? How does it happen in practice?
Questions often start
“How do… experience…”, “What do… perceive as…”, “How is… represented…”
Data
Words and images: interviews, observations, documents, media.
Sample
Smaller, chosen on purpose for depth and relevance.
You can claim
Rich insight into meanings, experiences and practices in a particular context.

Mixed methods

Combine numbers and stories, on purpose.

Asks
What happened, and why? Do the numbers and people’s accounts tell the same story?
Questions often start
“How do… accounts help to explain the… results?”
Data
Both numbers and words, collected and connected by design.
Sample
Often a larger group plus a smaller subgroup for depth.
You can claim
A fuller picture than either approach alone, if you plan how the two strands connect.
