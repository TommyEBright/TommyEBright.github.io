Before you film: read this first (2 minutes)
  • Film in slow motion (240 fps if your phone has it). Normal video is too jumpy for contact times.
  • Film side-on with the phone kept still (on a tripod or a bag), roughly at hip height and pointing straight across the lane.
  • Put flat markers on the line the runner runs along (e.g. tape or discs at 0, 5 and 10 m), not cones at the side of the lane. Measure them with a tape. Cones at the side are closer to or further from the camera than the runner, which makes the timing wrong (parallax).
  • Lay a scale on the ground in the running lane (e.g. two markers exactly 2 m apart) so the tool can measure step length.
  • Don't move or zoom the phone during the run, and make sure you can see at least five foot contacts.

How to mark accurately
  • Use ← and → to move one frame at a time, then click on the video to place the mark. A magnifier helps you aim.
  • Distance markers: use the hip every time, because it sits close to the centre of mass. Go to the frame where the hip is directly above the marker. Resting the mouse on the marker shows a yellow vertical guide line to help.
  • Touchdown is the first frame the foot touches the ground. Toe-off is the first frame the foot is off the ground. Always click the tip of the shoe.

    1. Find a moment in the video, e.g. a touchdown. Tip: click any touchdown or toe-off in the Step 2 list to jump straight to it.
    2. Pick its name below and press Add this frame. A small picture of it appears underneath.
    3. Measure angles (optional): press a button under the picture (e.g. Knee flexion), then click the body points the tool asks for on the video.
    4. Repeat for 3–6 moments. The sequence goes into your Word document, or you can download it as a picture.
    How the angles are measured
    • Knee flexion (hip, knee, ankle): 0° is a straight leg and bigger means more bent. It is 180° minus the angle between thigh and shank.
    • Hip angle (shoulder, hip, knee): the thigh relative to the line of the trunk. Positive is flexion (thigh in front of the trunk line) and negative is extension (thigh behind it).
    • Ankle angle (knee, ankle, tip of the shoe): the angle between shank and foot. Bigger means more plantarflexed (toes pointed), smaller means more dorsiflexed.
    • Trunk lean (hip, shoulder): the trunk relative to the vertical. Positive is leaning forward.
    • Shank angle (ankle, knee): the shin relative to the vertical. Positive means the knee is ahead of the ankle, negative means the foot is out in front of the knee.
    • Touchdown distance (hip, tip of the shoe, needs the ground scale): how far in front of the hip the foot lands. A big positive value usually means more braking.
    • The tool works out which way the runner is going so that "forward" is right. If your hip or trunk angles come out with the wrong sign, set it here:
    • If you set a ground scale, the tool uses it as the horizontal, so a slightly tilted phone does not change the angles. These are 2D angles, so they are only accurate if the camera is side-on to the movement.
