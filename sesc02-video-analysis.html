Kinematic Video Analyser

Linear and angular kinematics from your own phone video, in the browser. Your video never leaves your device.

Before you film: how to get video you can analyse
  • Side-on and square: the camera must point straight at the plane the athlete moves in (perpendicular to the direction of travel). This tool is 2D, so anything that moves towards or away from the camera will be measured wrongly.
  • Far back and zoomed in a little (e.g. 5–10 m away for a sprint). Standing close with a wide angle causes perspective error, especially near the edges of the picture.
  • Keep the phone still on a tripod or chair. Do not pan to follow the athlete, or the scale changes.
  • Put something of known length in the same plane as the athlete (e.g. a 1 m stick, or two cones 5 m apart on the running line). You calibrate with this.
  • Use slow-mo (120 or 240 fps) for sprinting and jumping. Good light helps keep the picture sharp.
  • Mark the joints with tape or stickers if you can (shoulder, hip, knee, ankle, toe), and wear fitted clothing.
  • iPhone: Settings → Camera → Formats → “Most Compatible”. Transfer by cable, AirDrop or OneDrive, not WhatsApp or email (they compress the video and can drop frames).

Then work through the tabs on the right in order: Setup → Calibrate → Digitise → Events → Results. Your work is saved in this browser as you go, and you can also save a project file.

How the numbers are calculated
  1. Calibration: pixels are converted to metres using your known length. y is up, x is forwards.
  2. Filtering: coordinates are smoothed with a 4th-order zero-lag Butterworth low-pass filter (a 2nd-order filter run forwards and backwards, cut-off adjusted for the two passes; Winter, 2009). Clicking errors are small in position but get much bigger when you work out velocity and acceleration, so this step matters. Untick “Filter” to see the raw data. Filtering also rounds off very sudden changes (e.g. at take-off or foot contact), so peak values can come out slightly lower. Compare filtered and raw, and say which you used.
  3. Velocity and acceleration: first and second central differences, e.g. vi = (xi+1 − xi−1) ÷ (ti+1 − ti−1). The first and last points have no neighbour on one side, so they are left blank.
  4. Joint angles are the angle between two segments (e.g. knee = between thigh and shank), so 180° is straight. Segment angles are measured from vertical: trunk lean and shin angle are positive when the top end is forwards.
  5. Angular velocity is the rate of change of angle (°/s). For the knee, positive means extending (straightening), negative means flexing (bending).
  6. Jump height from flight time uses h = g × t² ÷ 8. It assumes the body is in the same position at take-off and landing (e.g. no tucking the legs up).
  7. Hip ≠ centre of mass. The hip is a handy stand-in for the centre of mass, but arm and trunk movements shift the real centre of mass.
