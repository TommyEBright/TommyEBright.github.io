What makes a screening protocol defensible?

In plain English: a screening protocol is a small set of tests (the battery) plus a clear plan for what you'll do with each result (the decision tree).

A test earns its place only if it's linked to your injury mechanism, measured well enough to trust, possible with the kit and time you have, and would actually change what you do.

The five principles

Relevant: linked directly to the mechanism.
Reliable: the error (MDC) is smaller than the change you care about.
Valid: it measures what you think it measures.
Feasible: you can run it with force platforms, 2D video or basic field kit, in the time you have.
Actionable: the result would change what you do.

Common pitfalls

Kitchen-sink screening: testing everything "just in case".
Over-relying on normative values: norms from a different population.
Ignoring measurement error: treating noise as change.
Assuming causation: linked to injury doesn't mean it causes injury.
No intervention pathway: a result with no plan attached.

Our kit for the practical

Force platforms: jumps, landings, isometric tests, balance.
2D video: frontal and side-on angles during landing, squatting, cutting or running.
Basic field equipment: e.g. tape measure, goniometer, stopwatch, cones.

Anything needing 3D motion capture, EMG or isokinetics can stay in your case study, but not in the battery you run.

Notice

  1. Safety comes first. Pain or not being able to complete the test sends the athlete to a clinician before any numbers are used.
  2. The MDC comes before the threshold. Try "Crossed the line, within MDC": the score has moved below 250 N, but by less than the MDC, so the tree treats it as no real change. Is that the right call?
  3. Every end point is an action, not "monitor" on its own.
Part 1

Filter your tests

In plain English: bring in every test from your chain, rate each one on the five principles, then decide whether to keep it. Aim for three or four tests. For every test you drop, write one line saying why. You'll need that for section 3 of the case study.

Rule check

    Discuss in your group

    1. Where does your threshold come from? Was that study done on athletes like yours (e.g. sex, age, sport, level)?
    2. Is your MDC from a between-session study? If it's from within one session, your real error is probably bigger.
    3. Who acts on each end point: a coach, a physio, you? Would they understand the action as written?
