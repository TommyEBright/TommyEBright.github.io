Reflective writing scaffold

Choose a model, write each stage, then check the balance. Strong reflection spends most of its words on analysis and what you will do next, not on describing what happened.

1. Which model suits you?

Answer three questions for a suggestion. You still need to justify your choice in the portfolio.

ModelStagesStrengthWatch out for

Driscoll (1994)3Short, flexible and action-focusedEasy to stay shallow without prompts
Schön (1983)2Separates thinking during and after the eventGives few prompts for structure

3. Check the balance

    Descriptive or critical? Two examples

    Descriptive: "I used MyFitnessPal to record the athlete's diet. It took a long time and some foods were hard to find."

    Critical: "Finding items in the app took longer than planned, so I accepted near matches for two home-cooked meals. This probably underestimated fat intake, which matters because Figure 4 is used to judge fat as a share of energy. Next time I would weigh the ingredients of composite dishes and enter them separately, and I would check database entries against food labels."

    The critical version explains why, links to the data, judges the impact and commits to a specific change.
