How this works

Photoplethysmography (PPG)

Each heartbeat pushes a pulse of blood into your fingertip. More blood absorbs more light, so the brightness the camera sees dips slightly with every beat. Pulse Lab samples the average red (or green) colour of the centre of each video frame, resamples it to 30 Hz, removes slow drift, band-pass filters it (0.7 to 3.5 Hz, zero-phase Butterworth) and finds each pulse peak, refined below the frame interval by parabolic interpolation.

PRV is not quite ECG HRV

ECG-derived HRV uses the electrical R wave. A camera measures the pulse arriving at the finger, so strictly this is pulse rate variability (PRV). The pulse transit time from heart to finger varies a little from beat to beat (with breathing and blood pressure), which adds variability that the ECG does not see.

Cleaning the data

Intervals shorter than 300 ms or longer than 2000 ms, or more than 20% away from the local median, are removed. RMSSD only uses differences between two neighbouring kept intervals. If more than 20% of beats are removed the reading is flagged as unreliable.

Limitations

  • Valid only at rest, seated or lying still.
  • Any movement of the finger or phone ruins the signal.
  • Camera frame rate (about 30 per second) limits timing precision to a few milliseconds.
  • PPG may read SDNN and RMSSD slightly higher than ECG, especially when HRV is low.
  • Cold hands, pressing too hard and poor light all reduce signal quality.
  • For education only. Not a medical device.
