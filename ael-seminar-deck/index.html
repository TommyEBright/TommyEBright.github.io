What is accentuated eccentric loading (AEL)?

Extra load on the way down (eccentric), removed before the way up (concentric).

The idea

In a countermovement jump, muscles first lengthen under load to stop the drop (eccentric), then shorten to drive the body up (concentric).

Muscles are stronger eccentrically, so a normal jump never fully loads the lowering phase. AEL adds load just for that phase.

Why it might help

  • A bigger, faster stretch in the lowering phase
  • More force and stored elastic energy at the bottom of the jump (the stretch-shortening cycle)
  • A lighter body to push up once the load is dropped

Review: Wagle et al. (2017), Sports Medicine, 47, 2473–2495.

How it is used

  • Acutely: to try to boost jump performance in that session
  • As training: to develop eccentric strength and power over weeks

Eccentric = muscle lengthening under load (the lowering or braking phase); concentric = muscle shortening (the pushing up phase). Muscles are stronger eccentrically, so in a normal jump the lowering phase is under-loaded relative to what the muscle could tolerate.

AEL loads the eccentric phase more heavily than the concentric phase: here, by holding dumbbells on the way down and releasing them at the lowest point. Proposed benefits include greater eccentric force, a stronger stretch-shortening cycle and enhanced concentric output. The evidence is mixed and depends on the load and the person, which is exactly why we test it.

Use the chart: compare the load on the body in each phase for a normal jump, a jump holding the dumbbells throughout (LOADED) and an AEL jump. Ask: which condition should jump highest, and why?

Wagle, J. P., et al. (2017). Accentuated eccentric loading for training and performance: a review. Sports Medicine, 47(12), 2473–2495.

The jump, phase by phase

Pick a condition, then press Play or Step. Watch what the force plate records.

The force plate measures the force between the feet and the ground, sampled 1000 times a second. While the participant stands still it reads their weight (plus the dumbbells if held).

Unweighting: the participant starts to drop and the force falls below body weight. Braking: they slow the drop and the force rises above body weight. In AEL the dumbbells are released at the lowest point, so the plate force drops by the dumbbells' weight and the participant pushes up with only their own body mass. Hands go to the hips for take-off and landing. Flight: the plate reads zero. Jump height can be calculated from the flight time, or from take-off velocity found by integrating force.

The heights in this animation are the same in every condition on purpose: working out what really happens is the students' job.

Our AEL study, and your task

Explore the design, then predict the results before you work them out in Excel.

Design: 39 adults (27 men, 12 women) tested in two university labs. Every participant completed every condition (a within-participant, repeated-measures design), with three trials per condition. The no arm swing and arm swing jumps were recorded in two separate sessions, so we can also ask how repeatable they are.

Conditions: NAS = no arm swing, hands on hips; AS = arm swing; AEL_20/30/40 = dumbbells of about 20, 30 or 40% of body weight released at the bottom of the countermovement; LOADED_20/30/40 = the same dumbbells held throughout.
