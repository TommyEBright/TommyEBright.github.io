Before you film: how to get a flight you can analyse
  • Side-on and square. Point the camera straight at the plane of the throw or jump (the plane of motion). This is a 2D tool, so anything moving towards or away from the camera is measured wrongly.
  • Far back, zoomed in a little (e.g. 8 to 10 m away for a free throw). Close up with a wide angle gives perspective error, worst near the edges of the picture. Keep the camera level, because heights are measured from the floor.
  • Keep the camera still on a tripod. Do not follow the ball, or the scale changes.
  • Film something of known length in the same plane as the flight (e.g. a 1 m stick held upright where the athlete stands). You calibrate with it.
  • Get the whole flight in shot if you can, from a few frames before release to landing (or the hoop). If not, the first 8 to 10 frames after release are the minimum.
  • High frame rate and plenty of light (60 fps at least; 120 or 240 fps slow motion is better), so the ball is sharp in every frame. For slow motion, note the frame rate it was filmed at.
  • Note the details: which shots went in, and anything you need later (e.g. where the hoop is, or for a jump, where the toes and heels were).
