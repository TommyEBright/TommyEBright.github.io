Linear Kinematics of a Sprint

Position, velocity and acceleration over 30 m, worked through like your Practical Assessment.

The scenario

Two athletes, A and B, run 30 m from a standing start. Timing gates every 5 m give their split times. Who is faster, where, and why?

Step 1

Read the graph

A position–time graph shows where an athlete is (their displacement) at each moment. You can read velocity and acceleration from its shape without any maths.

Two rules (Bartlett, 2007):

The steepness (slope) of a position–time graph tells you the velocity. Steeper means faster.

The bend (curvature) tells you the acceleration. Curving upwards means speeding up, curving downwards means slowing down, and a straight line means velocity is constant and acceleration is zero.

Step 2

Calculate velocity and acceleration

The variables you need

VariableWhat it meansWhat usually happens in a sprint
Displacement (d)How far along the track the athlete is, in a straight line from the start (m).Keeps increasing down the track.
Velocity (v)How fast displacement changes. v = Δd ÷ Δt (m/s).Rises sharply from 0 at the start, then levels off near top speed.
Acceleration (a)How fast velocity changes. a = Δv ÷ Δt (m/s²).Highest in the first few metres, then drops towards zero as velocity levels off.
Step lengthDistance covered in one step, from one foot's contact to the other foot's contact (m).Gets longer from the start (around 1 m) up to top speed.
Step frequencyNumber of steps per second (Hz). Step frequency = 1 ÷ step time.High early on, then steadies or drops slightly as steps get longer.
Contact timeHow long the foot is on the ground in each step (s).Gets shorter as the athlete speeds up.
Flight timeHow long the body is in the air between steps (s).Gets longer as speed and step length increase.

Step or stride? A stride is two steps (from one foot landing to the same foot landing again). We use steps here because they are easier to measure from video.

These patterns are for a full sprint from blocks, where top speed is reached at around 30–60 m. Our athletes start standing and run 30 m, so they level off sooner.

How it works

Position (displacement) is how far along the track the athlete is, here 0, 5, 10… 30 m.

Velocity is how fast position changes. v = Δd ÷ Δt (change in position ÷ change in time), in m/s.

Acceleration is how fast velocity changes. a = Δv ÷ Δt (change in velocity ÷ change in time), in m/s².

Each velocity is an average over a 5 m split, so we plot it at the middle time of that split. Acceleration uses the time between those middle points.

Step 4

Interpret and apply

Numbers on their own are not an analysis. Use your tables and graphs to tell the story, and link it back to the phases from last week.

Worked example: a finished linear kinematic analysis of Athlete C (open this first)

This shows what a complete analysis looks like, set out the same way as your Practical Assessment. Athlete C is a different athlete, so use it for the structure, not the answers for A and B.

Task: 30 m sprint from a standing start. Timing gates every 5 m, and side-on video for step data.

1. Observe

Each step was split into stance (braking, propulsion) and swing (early, mid, late). In the first 10 m, C stands up tall after only two or three steps, and in the braking phase the foot lands well in front of the hip. From 10 m onwards, the foot lands closer to under the hip and the knee drives forwards strongly in mid swing.

3. Interpret
  • The start is the weak point. C takes 1.20 s to cover the first 5 m (an average of only 4.17 m/s), which is slow for an athlete who goes on to reach over 8 m/s.
  • Early steps are short with long ground contacts. Over 0–10 m, step length is only 1.20 m and contact time is 0.170 s. Landing with the foot in front of the hip means more braking, so more of each contact is spent slowing down rather than pushing forwards. Standing up early also points the push upwards rather than forwards.
  • Top speed is a strength. From 10 m onwards, step length grows to 1.96 m, contact time falls to 0.115 s and flight time rises to 0.130 s. C is still accelerating at 25–30 m and reaches 8.06 m/s, so C finishes well despite the slow start.
4. Apply

Focus: the first 10 m, especially braking and propulsion in the stance phase.

Cue: “Push the track away behind you” (six words), so each early step pushes back rather than reaching forwards.

How to check it worked: re-measure the 0–5 m time and the 0–10 m contact time and step length. If the cue works, the 0–5 m time should fall, contact time should drop and step length should increase.
