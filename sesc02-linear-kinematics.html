Linear Kinematics of a Sprint

Position, velocity and acceleration over 30 m, worked through like your Practical Assessment.

The scenario

Two athletes, A and B, run 30 m from a standing start. Timing gates every 5 m give their split times. Who is faster, where, and why?

Step 1

Read the graph

This graph shows an athlete's position (displacement) along the track during a 30 m sprint. You can read a lot from it without any maths.

Two rules (Bartlett, 2007):

The steepness (slope) of a position–time graph tells you the velocity. Steeper means faster.

The bend (curvature) tells you the acceleration. If the line is curving upwards, the athlete is speeding up. If it is straight, velocity is constant and acceleration is zero.

Step 2

Calculate velocity and acceleration

The variables you need

VariableWhat it meansWhat usually happens in a sprint
Displacement (d)How far along the track the athlete is, in a straight line from the start (m).Keeps increasing down the track.
Velocity (v)How fast displacement changes. v = Δd ÷ Δt (m/s).Rises sharply from 0 at the start, then levels off near top speed.
Acceleration (a)How fast velocity changes. a = Δv ÷ Δt (m/s²).Highest in the first few metres, then drops towards zero as velocity levels off.
Step lengthDistance covered in one step, from one foot's contact to the other foot's contact (m).Gets longer from the start (around 1 m) up to top speed.
Step frequencyNumber of steps per second (Hz). Step frequency = 1 ÷ step time.High early on, then steadies or drops slightly as steps get longer.
Contact timeHow long the foot is on the ground in each step (s).Gets shorter as the athlete speeds up.
Flight timeHow long the body is in the air between steps (s).Gets longer as speed and step length increase.

Step or stride? A stride is two steps (from one foot landing to the same foot landing again). We use steps here because they are easier to measure from video.

These patterns are for a full sprint from blocks, where top speed is reached at around 30–60 m. Our athletes start standing and run 30 m, so they level off sooner.

How it works

Position (displacement) is how far along the track the athlete is, here 0, 5, 10… 30 m.

Velocity is how fast position changes. v = Δd ÷ Δt (change in position ÷ change in time), in m/s.

Acceleration is how fast velocity changes. a = Δv ÷ Δt (change in velocity ÷ change in time), in m/s².

Each velocity is an average over a 5 m split, so we plot it at the middle time of that split. Acceleration uses the time between those middle points.
