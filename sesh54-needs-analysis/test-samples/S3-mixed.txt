Introduction
A needs analysis identifies the demands of a sport and the gap between those demands and the athlete, so that tests are chosen for a reason [REF: needs analysis framework]. This lightweight sculler struggles to hold pace across the race distance and has ten weeks until the World Championships.

Athlete profile
He is a 28-year-old international lightweight who must stay under 72.5 kg. He has six years in the national squad, which suggests a long training age.

Performance demands
Race performance in lightweight rowers is strongly related to aerobic power [REF: lightweight rowing physiology]. However, energy-system estimates for rowing vary with the method used [REF: rowing energy contribution methods]. Elite rowers produce high power outputs of [X] W during racing [REF: heavyweight rowing power]. For him, holding pace suggests that threshold power, rather than peak power, may be the limiter.

Test selection
An incremental step test on the ergometer is selected to identify power at the lactate threshold, which reflects the ability to hold race pace [REF: rowing step test validity]. The test has good test-retest reliability in trained rowers [REF: rowing step test reliability]. A 2000 m test will also be used. It has limitations.

Monitoring
The step test will be repeated in week six. Body mass will be recorded every morning. Training intensities will be adjusted using the threshold power from each test.

Constraints
The regatta in week four means that maximal testing should be avoided in week four. Making weight could affect performance in tests if he is dehydrated.

Conclusion
In conclusion, the priority is threshold power relative to body mass.
