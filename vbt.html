Velocity-Based Training

SESD56 · Week 11 · Build and use a load–velocity profile

Today's big idea: every lifter has their own straight line between load and bar speed. Build that line properly and it tells you what any load means for that person, today.

Your numbers

In plain English

The line

Heavier loads move slower, in a straight line that belongs to this lifter. Any point on the line answers: "if I put this load on the bar, how fast should it move today?"

Slope

How much bar speed you lose for every extra 10 kg. A steep slope means speed drops off quickly as the bar gets heavier.

V0: the speed end

Where the line hits the velocity axis: how fast the bar would move with nothing on it. Think of it as the lifter's speed quality. It's extrapolated, so treat it as an idea, not a number you'd ever see.

L0: the strength end

Where the line hits the load axis: the load the lifter couldn't move at all. Think of it as their strength ceiling. It's always heavier than a real 1RM, because a real lift fails before the bar stops completely.

Estimated 1RM, with a health warning

Follow the line down to the velocity a lifter usually has at their 1RM (around 0.3 m/s for a full back squat) and read off the load. Quick, safe and no max testing, but:

  • It's an extrapolation beyond the heaviest load you lifted. The further you go, the bigger the error.
  • The velocity at 1RM differs between people and from day to day. Change the box above by 0.05 m/s and watch the estimate move.
  • Near maximum the relationship isn't always straight, and depth, pause and technique changes shift every point.
  • Today's 1RM guess set your loads, so a bad guess gives you a squashed line.
The textbook warns against using the profile to estimate 1RM. Use it as a ballpark for setting loads, never as a test result in your portfolio without saying how uncertain it is.

Compare your two lifters

What could a difference mean for training?

A lifter with a high V0 but a lower L0 is relatively quick but runs out of strength: more heavy strength work may help. A lifter with a high L0 but a low V0 is strong but slow off the mark: more speed–strength work may help. These are pointers for a conversation, not rules. Two lifters with the same 1RM can have very different lines.

Use the profile to choose a load for today. Pick a lifter and a zone, put on the load the line suggests, then let the first rep tell you whether to adjust.

Zones are practitioner guidance, not exact thresholds. If today's first rep is faster than your line says, the lifter is moving well today; if it's slower, they might be tired. That day-to-day check is the main practical benefit of VBT.

Stop the set by velocity loss

Do one set at the zone load. Stop when a rep is slower than the stop velocity. Type each rep as you go.
