Quick check: which velocity are we using?

Mean velocity is the average bar speed over the whole upward phase. It's what we'll use today. Some studies report mean propulsive velocity, which only covers the part where the lifter is still pushing the bar up. At light loads the two differ, because the bar slows down near the top. So only compare numbers measured the same way.

Task 2

Why build a load–velocity profile?

The idea in one line

The heavier the bar, the slower it moves. If you measure how fast someone lifts a few different loads, you get a line that is unique to that person and that exercise.

What it lets you do

  • Prescribe: find the load that gives any target speed.
  • Compare: see whether someone is relatively faster or stronger.
  • Monitor: retest and see whether the line has moved.

Make sense of it

What do different results mean?

Enter at least three loads for a lifter to see an explanation of their line.

Reading the line

  • Height of the line. A line that sits higher means faster at every load.
  • Slope. A steep line means speed drops quickly as load is added. A flat line means the lifter holds speed better with heavy loads.
  • Where it hits the velocity axis (V0). The theoretical speed with no load, which is the “speed end” of the profile.
  • Where it hits the load axis (L0). The theoretical load at zero speed, which is the “force end”. It is not a 1RM.

Using it properly

  • Profiles are usually very straight (r² close to 1) when every rep is at maximal intent. A messy line usually means a technique, depth or effort problem.
  • The textbook warns that using the profile to estimate 1RM tends to overestimate it and can lead to loads that are too heavy. Test 1RM directly if you need it.
  • Retest on another day under the same conditions. If the line has moved up or to the right by more than the day-to-day noise, the lifter has improved.

Haff (Ed.), Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning (5th ed.), Chapter 14 (Load–velocity profile). Weakley et al. (2021), Velocity-based training: from theory to application, Strength and Conditioning Journal, 43(2), 31–49.

Reality check from the research

  • In 80 strength-trained men doing a full squat, mean velocity at 1RM was 0.32 ± 0.03 m/s, and velocity was very closely linked to %1RM (Sánchez-Medina et al., 2017).
  • In 352 young recreational athletes (full squat, mean propulsive velocity), men lifted 70% 1RM at 0.61 ± 0.09 m/s and women at 0.55 ± 0.10 m/s. The authors note that their values at 50% 1RM were about the same as those reported at 70% 1RM in trained athletes, and that velocity at each %1RM varied a lot between people (Fernandez Ortega et al., 2022).
  • Because of this spread, those authors recommend working out the load–velocity relationship individually for people who aren't strength-trained (Fernandez Ortega et al., 2022).

The take-home: the same velocity can mean a different %1RM for different people, exercises and devices. Use the zones as a guide, and use each lifter's own profile to set their loads.

Haff (Ed.), Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning (5th ed.), Table 14.13. Sánchez-Medina et al. (2017), Sports Medicine International Open, 1, E80–E88. Fernandez Ortega et al. (2022), International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(11), 6756.

Train in a zone

How to run it

  1. Choose a zone and pick a starting load (use your profile if you built one in Task 2).
  2. Do one fast rep and enter its mean velocity.
  3. Adjust the load until the first rep lands in the zone, then do the set.
  4. Discuss: would a %1RM from last month have given the same load today?
Task 4

Velocity loss: when should the set stop?

As fatigue builds, each rep slows down. Velocity loss is how much slower the current rep is than the fastest rep in the set. Coaches set a limit (e.g. 20%) and end the set once a rep goes past it.

Try this

Same lifter, same load, two sets on different stations or after full rest: one stopped at 10% loss and one at 30%. Compare the reps done and how each set felt. Which would you use before a match, and which in an off-season hypertrophy block?

What the research found

  • Over 8 weeks of squat training, stopping sets at 20% velocity loss gave similar strength gains to 40%, and bigger improvements in CMJ (9.5% vs 3.5%), despite about 40% fewer reps.
  • The 40% group got more growth in two of the quadriceps muscles, but also lost some of their fastest (type IIX) muscle fibres.
  • A 2025 study found the best strength gains with a moderate (20%) limit compared with 40%.

Pareja-Blanco et al. (2017), Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 27(7), 724–735. Sánchez-Valdepeñas et al. (2025), Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 35(6), e70090.

Task 5

Could your phone do this?

A GymAware is expensive. Several phone apps use the camera to track the bar and give you mean velocity. Your clients won't have a GymAware, but they will have a phone. So is a phone good enough?

What the research says

  • A 2026 systematic review found that phone apps are less valid and less reliable than dedicated devices, and that results varied between apps, exercises and loads. Its conclusion was that they are suitable for recreational and applied settings, but not for high-precision research.
  • In competitive powerlifters filmed alongside 3D motion capture, one app (Qwik VBT) was as accurate as a linear position transducer. Two others (Metric VBT and MyLift) were less accurate and missed reps (52 and 175 of 589). In that study the phone was on a stand 1 m high, 3 m from the end of the bar.
  • When Metric was tested against a GymAware, agreement was good for the squat, but poorer for the bench press and deadlift.
  • Apps update often, so a validation study of an older version may not apply to the version on your phone.

Puppyn & Brughelli (2026), International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching, doi:10.1177/17479541261447622. Renner et al. (2024), PLOS ONE, 19(11), e0313919. Puppyn et al. (2025), International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching, doi:10.1177/17479541251391967.
