Integration: from acceleration to velocity to position

SESC02 Mechanics of Human Movement · working out jump motion from the area under a curve

1. The big idea: area, not slope

In Linear Kinematics you went down the chain by finding the slope (differentiation). Integration goes back up the chain by finding the area under the curve.

Position (m)
slope ↓
area ↑
Velocity (m/s)
slope ↓
area ↑
Acceleration (m/s²)
Area under acceleration–time = change in velocity.   Area under velocity–time = change in position.
Notice the word change. Integration tells you how much something has changed, so you always need to know where it started.

2. Area by hand: the trapezoid rule

Real data are not neat straight lines, so we cut the area into thin slices and add them up. Each slice is a trapezoid: its area is the average of the two heights × the width.

Section displacement = ½ × (vstart + vend) × Δt   ·   Height so far = previous height + section displacement

3. Where do you start? The starting value

Integration gives the change in velocity, not the velocity itself. To get velocity you need the starting velocity at the moment you begin adding up area:

velocity = starting velocity + area under acceleration–time

Countermovement and squat jumps
The jumper stands (or squats) completely still on the platform before moving, so the starting velocity is 0 m/s. That is why the still period at the start matters so much.
Drop jumps
The jumper steps off a box and is already falling when they land. At contact they are moving downwards at v = −√(2 × 9.81 × box height). Start at zero and every velocity after that is wrong.

5. Small errors grow

Because integration keeps adding, any small error is added again at every sample. An error in acceleration grows into a bigger error in velocity, and a bigger one again in position.
