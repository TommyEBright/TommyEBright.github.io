Paper Ball Challenge

SESD52 · Performance and Technique Analysis for Sport · work in pairs

Notational analysis. Take 10 shots each from the line. While one of you throws, the other taps Goal or Miss and films every shot on their phone (side-on, whole body in view, slow-mo if you have it). Then swap.

Shoot and record

Finish all 10 shots for both throwers to see who won.

And the winner is…

It’s a draw! Sudden death. Take one shot each until one of you scores and the other misses. Who won?

Your notation tells you what happened. It can’t tell you why. For that you need the video and some biomechanics.

Compare technique

Put the winner’s and loser’s videos side by side. Pause just before release and step through frame by frame. For each statement, tap who it describes best. Open Why it matters if you’re not sure what to look for.

Your summary

Share with the class

Pick one difference and the biomechanical principle behind it (e.g. “More consistent release speed, because distance is very sensitive to release speed”).

Critique your method

  • Is 10 shots enough to say who is really the better thrower? What would happen with 50?
  • Could your phone’s frame rate capture the exact moment of release?
  • Were you judging technique by eye? How could you measure it instead (e.g. release angle in Kinovea)?

That’s your Case Study in miniature. Notation → video analysis → biomechanical principles → recommendations.
