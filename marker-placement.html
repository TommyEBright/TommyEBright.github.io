Markers build the skeleton

In the static trial, QTM uses the bony landmark markers to build each segment: where the joint centres are and which way the axes point. Every trial after that uses those axes.

So if a knee marker is 1 cm too far forward, the knee’s flexion axis is tilted for every trial. Part of the big flexion movement then shows up as fake ab/adduction or rotation.

Big angles are robust, small angles are fragile. Knee flexion barely changes. Knee valgus and rotation can change completely.

Same knee, same movement

Researchers moved the knee landmarks by 9–18 mm and recalculated the angles three times. Nothing else changed.

The same paper found that clearer landmark definitions made people palpating the same femur agree more closely. That is why each marker in this tool has a written definition.

Six golden rules

  1. Feel for bone with your fingertips, not your eyes. Ask the participant to move the joint to help.
  2. Put the marker on the bone, not on the soft tissue next to it.
  3. One person palpates, one checks against the definition.
  4. Check left and right look symmetrical (e.g. both knees at the same height).
  5. Tight, minimal clothing. Markers on skin, not on loose fabric.
  6. Check the static trial in QTM before you record anything else.

Think about it

  1. Which marker would cause the biggest problem in a CMJ if it was 1 cm out? Why?
  2. Why can a tracking marker or cluster sit “anywhere sensible” on a segment, but an epicondyle marker cannot?
  3. The medial knee and ankle markers are taken off after the static trial. How does QTM still know where the knee and ankle are?
