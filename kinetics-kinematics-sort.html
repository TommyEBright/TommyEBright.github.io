Depth jump analyser

Add one depth jump from the force platform and work through it one step at a time. It uses the same method as the Excel template, based on McMahon et al. (2021).

Before you start, check your trial. The platform must be empty for at least half a second at the start. The jumper must land back on the platform and stand completely still for the last second or more. If either is missing, the numbers will be wrong, so collect another trial.

Add your data

Open the text file you saved from BioWare, or copy the time and vertical force (Fz) columns from Excel and paste them in the box.

1 Look at the raw force

This is your vertical force against time. Before you go any further, find where the jumper touches down, takes off and lands again. Why is the force zero at the start, and again in the middle?

2 Body weight

With one platform, the jumper starts on the box, so we cannot weigh them at the start. We use the last second of the trial, when they are standing still on the platform. Body weight is the average force in that second. Body mass is body weight divided by 9.81.

3 Touchdown, take-off and landing

The platform is never perfectly at zero when it is empty, so we use a small force threshold (10 N unless you changed it). Touchdown is the first sample above it. Take-off is the first sample back below it. Landing is the next sample above it.

4 Net force and acceleration

Net force is force minus body weight. It is what speeds the jumper up or slows them down. Acceleration is net force divided by body mass (Newton's second law). In flight the acceleration should sit at about -9.81 m/s/s. If it does, your body weight is right.

5 Velocity, and how far they really dropped

Velocity is the running total of acceleration over time, starting at touchdown. The jumper is already moving when they hit the platform, so we need a starting velocity. First guess: use the box height. Then check it. The jumper is standing still at the end, so velocity there should be zero. Whatever it reads instead is the error in our first guess, so we take it away.

6 Displacement, braking and propulsion

Displacement is the running total of velocity over time. It is zero at touchdown, so it shows how far the centre of mass moves down and back up. The moment velocity crosses zero is the lowest point. Braking is touchdown to that point. Propulsion is that point to take-off.

7 Your results

Now explain it. Go back to your deterministic model. Which of these numbers sit in which boxes? If you compare two box heights or two instructions, which box changed first, and what did that do to the ones above it?

Method: McMahon, J.J., Lake, J.P., Stratford, C. and Comfort, P. (2021) A proposed method for evaluating drop jump performance with one force platform. Biomechanics, 1(2), 178-189. Two things differ from the paper. Touchdown, take-off and landing use a fixed force threshold, and reactive strength index is added.
