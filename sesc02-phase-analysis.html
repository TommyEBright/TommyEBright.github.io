Qualitative Phase Analysis

Split a movement into phases, time them and turn what you see into a coaching cue.

Movement

Step 1

Phase table

Each phase has its own job and starts and ends at a clear moment you can spot on video (a key event). Watch your clip, find each key event, then fill in what you see in that phase.

Remember: phases help you look closely, but the movement still works as one whole. Think about how each phase sets up the next one.

Step 2

Timings

Scroll through your video and note the time (in seconds) at each key event, e.g. 1.230. The tool does the maths.

Using slow-mo playback on a phone? The clip plays slower than real life, so the times on screen are stretched. Enter how many times slower it plays (often 8 for 240 fps, 4 for 120 fps). If you are using Quintic or Kinovea, leave this as 1.

For each step: touchdown and toe-off of one foot, then touchdown of the other foot.

Step 3

Coaching cue

Pick one thing you would change and turn it into a cue the athlete could use straight away.

A good cue is short (six words or fewer), says what to do rather than what not to do, and makes it clear which body part does it and how they will know it worked (Bartlett, 2007).
