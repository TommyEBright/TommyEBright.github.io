Two lenses on one study: confidence vs credible intervals

Each simulated study gives a frequentist 95% confidence interval (blue) and a Bayesian 95% credible interval (teal). Change the prior and the sample size, and watch how the two lenses differ.

Predict first. If you use a strong prior that is wrong (centred far from the truth), what happens to the credible intervals when n = 10? And when n = 200?
Check your prediction
With n = 10 the posterior is pulled towards the wrong prior, so the credible intervals sit off-target and many miss the true value. With n = 200 the data pull the intervals much closer to the truth, but a prior this confident (SD 1.5) still drags many of them off target. Make the prior weaker (SD 5 or more) and the two lenses almost coincide. A credible interval describes what you should believe given the prior and the data. It is not built to capture the true value 95% of the time across repeated studies, which is what a confidence interval is designed for.
