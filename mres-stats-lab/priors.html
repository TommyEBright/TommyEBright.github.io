Choosing priors: prior predictive checks and sensitivity analysis

Model: BMI = α + β × (steps − 8.6k) + error, fitted to the JASP Physical Activity and BMI data. Choose priors, see what they imply before the data, then see how much they matter after it.

Predict first. A "vague" prior such as β ~ Normal(0, 100) feels safe and objective. What kind of BMI values does it predict? And does it change the posterior for the slope, or the Bayes factor, more?
Check your prediction
The vague prior predicts BMIs of −500 and +600, which is absurd for human beings. With 100 participants, the posterior for the slope hardly changes, because the data dominate. The Bayes factor, however, falls dramatically as the prior gets wider: a vague H1 spreads its bets over impossible effects and is penalised for it. Use weakly informative priors that respect the scale of your measures, check them with a prior predictive check, and report a sensitivity analysis.

What to take away (WAMBS checklist, Depaoli & van de Schoot, 2017)

  • Understand the prior: what values does it make plausible, on the scale of your outcome? A prior predictive check makes this visible.
  • Report and justify every prior: its distribution, its parameters and where they came from (theory, literature, expert elicitation, or weakly informative defaults).
  • Check prior–data conflict: if the likelihood sits far in the prior's tail, ask why before trusting the posterior.
  • Run a sensitivity analysis: refit with wider or narrower priors and report whether your conclusions change. Bayes factors usually need this far more than posterior estimates do.
